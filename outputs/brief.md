# Container Ports and Shipping Connectivity, 2015–2019

**Summary:** China leads the world on both container traffic and liner shipping connectivity, and countries' rankings on the two measures are closely aligned. Growth and decline were uneven, and a few countries, including Finland, Indonesia and France, sit at clearly different positions on the two measures.

## Findings

**Scope.** 2019 is the latest year with data for both indicators, so the comparison covers 2015–2019.

**Leaders.** China handled by far the most container traffic, 237,570,000 TEU (standard container units). It also has the best connectivity index, 158.6.

**Fastest growth.**
- Qatar's container traffic grew 135.85% between 2015 and 2019, the fastest among countries with at least 500,000 TEU at the start.
- Qatar's connectivity index also grew fastest, by 225.33%, though from a low base.

**Declines.**
- Venezuela's traffic fell the most, by 56.71%.
- Bahrain's connectivity fell the most, by 56.56%.

**Nordic countries.**
- Sweden is the best connected Nordic country, with an index of 48.96. That is well behind the global leaders.
- Finland moves about as much container traffic as Sweden, but its index is only 14.79.
- Norway, Denmark and Iceland have low-to-moderate connectivity. Their traffic is rising.

**Different positions on the two indicators.**
- Indonesia is among the largest container handlers, but its connectivity index is only moderate, at 45.66. It ranks much better on traffic than on connectivity.
- France handled 4,992,918 TEU, a mid-sized volume, yet its connectivity is relatively well developed. It ranks better on connectivity than on traffic.
- Finland, described above, is another example.

**Overall alignment.** The rank correlation (Spearman) between the two indicators in 2019 is 0.924. Countries that handle more containers generally also have better liner connections.

## Limitations

- Container traffic data stop at 2019. Connectivity data continue to 2021, but those later years cannot be compared with traffic.
- Growth rates exclude small-base countries (under 500,000 TEU, or a connectivity index under 10). Qatar's connectivity growth still starts from a low base.
- Differences between the rankings were judged from the top-30 lists for 2019 and individual country series, not from a full ranking of all countries.
- The Nordic comments on Norway, Denmark and Iceland are general, not backed by separate findings.
- The data cover an unbalanced set of countries. They exclude landlocked and non-reporting countries. TEU figures depend on national reporting and were not checked for outliers. The comparability of the connectivity index across years is unverified.
- The correlation measures rank association only, not causation.
